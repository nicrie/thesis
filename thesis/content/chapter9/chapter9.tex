\section*{Outlook}

\paragraph{pattern identification in dense data sets}

immediate follow-up studies could focus on the identified teleconnection patterns from chapter 7. as a first step to solidify the identified patterns and describe the atmospheric processes involved that lead to these teleconnections. as a second step, these teleconnections could be used to improve predictions on sub-seasonal to decadal time scales.

Time-delayed Probabilistic Factor Analysis (reference) could pave the way forward for more probabilistic modelling of teleconnections. the major advantage of this model is that time lags are modelled explicitly (but not specifically! they are still data driven) rather than implicitly like in the Hilbert framework applied here.

\paragraph{pattern identification in scarce data sets}
Various follow-up applications are possible, including but not limited to spatial modelling of plastic pollution and biodiversity. The GPR framework can be applied in a variety of relevant environmental problems, where we have sparse data sets and even a different variety of data sources. For example, the need of harmonizing and compiling the information generated by the numerous initiatives to characterize macro-litter over the world. Global Litter Observatory (GLO) relies (i) on citizenship as a major global force for litter monitoring, and (ii) on the adoption of the mass-based metric to allow comparability among datasets, an important weakness in the current evaluation systems. GPR framework can be adapated to combined datasets from different sources, modelling the joint spatial distibution based on the differnt underlying accuracy of different data source. also ideal for combining citizen data with high-precision government data (follow up study planned; to be presented at EGU25). 

Similarly, GPs can be adapted to be used for classification instead of regression. in this case, the model could be used to predict occurence probability of different marine species in the north weastern mediterranean sea (MINKA database)


finally, theoretical models have been developed that allow the combination of Probabilistic PCA/Factor Analysis with Gaussian processes. This could be used to examine underlying patterns in the pan-European beach litter data set recently published (2023) to better udnerstand variability in marine plastc polution, e.g. historical trends etc.
