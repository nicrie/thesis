\chapter{Proofs} 

The Frobenius norm $\Vab{\cdot}_F$ of a complex $N\times P$ matrix $\bm{A} \in \mathtt{C}^{N \times P}$ is defined as:

\begin{align}\label{eq:app:frobenius_norm}
    \Vab{\bm{A}}_F &\equiv  \pab{\sum_i^N \sum_j^P \vab{a_{ij}}^2}^{1/2} = \pab{\sum_{i}^{\min(N, P)} \sigma_i^2(\bm{A})}^{1/2}.
\end{align}

This expression reveals that the Frobenius norm is equivalent to the $\ell_2$-norm of the matrix's singular values $\sigma_i$. We can demonstrate this equivalence using the singular value decomposition (SVD) of $\bm{A}$ and leveraging the property that the trace of a matrix remains invariant under cyclic permutations.

Starting with the defintion of the Frobenius norm, we have:

\begin{align*}
    \Vab{\bm{A}}_F &= \pab{\tr \pab{\bm{A}^H\bm{A}}}^{1/2}
\end{align*}

Applying the SVD, we can express $\bm{A} = \bm{V}\bm{\Sigma}\bm{V}^H$, leading to:

\begin{align*}
    \Vab{\bm{A}}_F &= \pab{\tr \pab{\bm{V}\bm{\Sigma}\bm{U}^H\bm{U}\bm{\Sigma}^H\bm{V}^H}}^{1/2}
\end{align*}

Since $\bm{U}^H\bm{U}=\bm{I}$, this simplifies to:

\begin{align*}
    \Vab{\bm{A}}_F &= \pab{\tr \pab{\bm{V}\bm{\Sigma}^2\bm{V}^H}}^{1/2}
\end{align*}


Finally using the invariance property of the trace, we obtain:

\begin{align*}
    \Vab{\bm{A}}_F &= \pab{\sum_{i}^{\min(N, P)} \sigma_i^2(\bm{A})}^{1/2}
\end{align*}

This concludes the proof that the Frobenius norm of a matrix is indeed the $\ell_2$-norm of its singular values.
